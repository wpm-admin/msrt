About Maserati 3500 GT Vignale Spyder

The Maserati 3500 GT Vignale Spyder is a 2-door roadster of the GT class that was available in coupe and convertible versions. This car was produced by the Italian company Maserati from 1957 to 1964. The 3500 GT was the first production Gran Turismo in the company’s history.

In 1958, an open version of the Touring Spyder was presented at the Turin Motor Show, but another Spyder, created by the Vignale atelier and designed by Giovanni Michelotti, entered the market. This car was presented at the 1959 Paris Motor Show. The new Maserati 3500 GT Vignale Spyder was a 2+2 convertible with a wheelbase shortened to 2500 mm. Its body was made primarily of steel, with the exception of the aluminum hood, trunk and removable hardtop. Among the options available for this Spyder was a hard top in a unique color that, when installed, created a spectacular roofline that made the car particularly attractive and desirable. The Maserati 3500GT Vignale Spyder is a true masterpiece that can compete in beauty with cars from other eras. This rare Vignale-bodied convertible was produced in only 232 units. It is powered by the original 3.5-liter inline six-cylinder engine with three Weber twin carburetors that ensure its excellent performance. The engine is mated to a 5-speed ZF transmission that provides smooth and precise power delivery. The car features a black leather interior with black carpets, just as it left the factory, front disc brakes and a stainless steel exhaust system.
